# Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind

> [!info] Book summary · by Yuval Noah Harari · read Feb 2020

![image](attachments/sapiens_sketchnote.webp)

## Summary

_Sapiens_ by Yuval Noah Harari traces the history of our species from the Cognitive Revolution to the modern era. Key themes from my highlights:

- **Imagined Realities:** Harari argues that Sapiens' unique power lies in our ability to create and believe in fictions (money, nations, laws, corporations). This allows for large-scale cooperation far beyond the "natural" gossip limit of 150 people.
- **The Agricultural Revolution as "Fraud":** While the transition to farming allowed for population explosions, it often resulted in harder work, poorer diets, and more individual suffering compared to hunter-gatherers. It was an "evolutionary success" but a "biochemical failure" for the individual.
- **Universal Unifiers:** The world has been unified by three universal orders: economic (money), political (empires), and religious. Money is highlighted as the most effective trust system ever created.
- **Credit and Growth:** The modern era is driven by capitalism and credit—the ability to build the present at the expense of a (hopefully) more abundant future.
- **Biochemical Happiness:** Happiness is described as a product of internal biochemistry and the alignment of our expectations with reality. Harari suggests that modern society may be "depleting the globe's reservoirs of contentment" by inflating expectations through media and advertising.
- **The End of Sapiens:** The book concludes by looking toward a future where genetic engineering and brain-computer interfaces may allow humans to transcend natural selection entirely.
