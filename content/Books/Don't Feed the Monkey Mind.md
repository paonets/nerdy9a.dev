# Don't Feed the Monkey Mind: How to Stop the Cycle of Anxiety, Fear, and Worry

![image](attachments/dont_feed_the_monkey_mind_sketchnote.webp)

## Summary

_Don't Feed the Monkey Mind_ offers a CBT-based approach to breaking the cycle of anxiety by changing how we respond to the "monkey mind." Key themes from my highlights:

- **The Monkey Mind-set:** This mindset is built on three pillars: **Intolerance of Uncertainty**, **Perfectionism**, and **Over-responsibility**. It thrives on "safety strategies" (avoidance, resistance, distraction) that provide temporary relief but guarantee more anxiety later.
- **Primordial Threats:** Our brains are wired to fear death and the loss of social status. The monkey mind often misinterprets modern social stresses as these ancient threats to survival.
- **Safety Strategies vs. Expansive Strategies:** Feeding the monkey with avoidance confirms that a threat is real. Instead, "Expansive Strategies" involve doing the opposite—welcoming uncertainty, allowing mistakes, and taking responsibility only for oneself.
- **Welcoming Negative Feelings:** Rather than trying to relax (a common safety strategy), we should treat anxiety as "necessary feelings." By welcoming the sensations without acting on them, we allow them to run their course.
- **Resilience Formula:** The book emphasizes that **Anxiety × Welcoming = Resilience**. Suffering is caused by resistance (Pain × Resistance = Suffering).
- **Values-Based Action:** Expansion is fueled by identifying values (like creativity, risk, or adventure) that are more important than the temporary feeling of safety.
