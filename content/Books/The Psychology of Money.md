# The Psychology of Money: Timeless lessons on wealth, greed, and happiness

![image](attachments/the_psychology_of_money_sketchnote.webp)

## Summary

_The Psychology of Money_ explores the emotional and behavioral aspects of finance, arguing that success with money is more about behavior than math. Key themes from my highlights:

- **Time as the Ultimate Return:** The highest form of wealth is the ability to wake up and say, "I can do whatever I want today." Controlling your time is a more dependable predictor of wellbeing than any objective condition of life.
- **Reasonable over Rational:** In the real world, people don't want the mathematically optimal strategy; they want the one that helps them sleep at night. Aiming to be "reasonable" minimizes future regret and helps you stay the course.
- **The Power of Tail Events:** Investing success is driven by "tails"—a tiny fraction of events or companies that provide the majority of returns. Investing genius is simply being able to do the "average thing" when everyone else is panicking.
- **Wealth is What You Don't See:** Wealth is the financial assets that haven't yet been converted into "stuff." It provides flexibility and options, which are unseen but high-value returns.
- **Survival is the Only Strategy:** To succeed, you must first survive. A margin of safety (like keeping cash) allows you to endure short-term volatility without being forced to sell your long-term assets.
- **Avoiding Single Points of Failure:** Progress is slow and compounding is quiet, but destruction is fast and loud. Resilience comes from avoiding single points of failure in your financial plan.
