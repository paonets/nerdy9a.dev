# The Little Book of Common Sense Investing

> [!info] Book summary · by John C. Bogle · read Dec 2023

![image](attachments/the_little_book_of_common_sense_investing_sketchnote.webp)

## Summary

_The Little Book of Common Sense Investing_ is the definitive guide to index investing, written by the founder of Vanguard. Key themes from my highlights:

- **Index Investing as the "Winner's Game":** Bogle argues that trying to beat the market is a loser's game for the vast majority. A low-cost index fund allows you to capture your "fair share" of market returns by eliminating security and manager selection risk.
- **The Three Sources of Stock Returns:** Returns are driven by **Dividend Yield**, **Earnings Growth** (together called "investment return"), and **P/E Multiple changes** ("speculative return").
- **Costs and Taxes Matter:** Over the long run, costs and taxes are the most significant factors in determining your final returns. Information about costs is "priceless" while past performance is often "meaningless."
- **Asset Allocation:** Bogle recommends a balanced approach between stocks and bonds to manage risk and prevent counterproductive emotional behavior (like panic-selling). He suggests simpler strategies (like 80/20 or 100% equity for young investors) over complex ones.
- **The Danger of Tactical Allocation:** Investors should generally avoid trying to time the market or vary their stock/bond ratio based on conditions, as this implies a "prescience" that few possess.
- **Diversification:** Total market diversification through an index fund leaves you only with "market risk," which is necessary for growth, while removing idiosyncratic risks.
