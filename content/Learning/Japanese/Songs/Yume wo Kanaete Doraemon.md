# 夢をかなえてドラえもん (Make My Dreams Come True, Doraemon) — Song Study & Grammar Breakdown

- **Title:** 夢をかなえてドラえもん (Yume wo Kanaete Doraemon — _Make My Dreams Come True, Doraemon_)
- **Vocal:** mao, with chorus by ひまわりキッズ (Himawari Kids)
- **Lyrics & Music:** 黒須克彦 (Katsuhiko Kurosu)
- **Arrangement:** 大久保薫 (Kaoru Okubo)
- **Anime:** _Doraemon_ (2005 series), opening theme from May 2007 to Sep 2019, and again from 2024 (episode 836 onward). mao's debut single (4 Jul 2007).
- **Full lyrics:** [Doraemon Wiki](https://doraemon.fandom.com/wiki/Yume_Wo_Kanaete_Doraemon) (this note quotes only short lines for study)
- **Hub:** [[Learning/Japanese/Songs/index|Japanese Songs Hub]]

## 🌟 Background & Emotional Landscape

For over a decade this was the song every Japanese child heard at the start of _Doraemon_. It replaced _ハグしちゃお_ (Hagushichao) in 2007, gave way to Gen Hoshino's _ドラえもん_ in 2019, and came back in 2024. A special version sung by the main voice cast was the theme of the 2015 film _Nobita's Space Heroes_.

The lyrics are written from a child's point of view, and the chorus speaks to Doraemon directly. Between lines, the Himawari Kids shout the names of his gadgets as call-and-response:

| Callout                                      | Gadget        | What it does                                      |
| :------------------------------------------- | :------------ | :------------------------------------------------ |
| タケコプター                                 | Bamboo-Copter | Propeller you stick on your head to fly           |
| どこでもドア                                 | Anywhere Door | Opens onto any place you name                     |
| タイムマシン                                 | Time Machine  | Travel through the desk drawer to past or future  |
| <ruby>四次元<rt>よじげん</rt></ruby>ポケット | 4D Pocket     | The bottomless pocket all the gadgets come out of |

### Core Philosophy:

> **「<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>になっても　きっと<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れない」**
> _"Even when I grow up, I surely won't forget."_

The song opens by worrying that adults forget their childhood dreams, and closes by promising not to. Doraemon's pocket stands for imagination itself.

### Why it is great for learners

Unlike the Ghibli songs in this hub, the language is plain, spoken, child-level Japanese. Almost every line is a textbook grammar pattern: `〜たら`, `〜ても`, `〜ないで`, volitional `〜よう`, `〜てあげる`, `〜ばいい`, and two causatives. It sits right at the IRODORI A2 level.

## 🎵 Key Lines & Analysis

### Verse 1: The Map of Dreams

> <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>をのせた　<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>だけの<ruby>世界地図<rt>せかいちず</rt></ruby>
> _A world map all my own, with my dreams placed on it_

#### 🔑 Vocab & Points

- **えがく (<ruby>描<rt>えが</rt></ruby>く - egaku):** To draw / picture in the mind. The verse opens with the singer always picturing this map in their heart (`えがいてる` = `えがいている`, casual drop of `い`).
- **のせた (<ruby>載<rt>の</rt></ruby>せた - noseta):** Placed on / carried. The same verb as [[Learning/Japanese/Songs/Kimi wo Nosete|君をのせて]], here meaning "dreams written onto the map". A relative clause: `夢をのせた` modifies `世界地図`.
- **<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>だけの (jibun dake no):** One's very own / only for oneself.
- **<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて (jikan wo koete):** Crossing over time (`越える` = to go beyond). The next lines list what the map allows: flying through the sky, crossing time, opening a door to a faraway country.

---

### Bridge 1: Will I Forget?

> <ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>になったら<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れちゃうのかな？
> _When I grow up, will I end up forgetting?_

#### 🔑 Vocab & Points

- **〜になったら (ni nattara):** "When / once (I) become…". `〜たら` for a future condition.
- **<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れちゃう (wasurechau):** Casual contraction of `忘れてしまう`: to forget completely, often with regret.
- **〜のかな (no kana):** "I wonder if…", a soft question to oneself. Compare the feminine `〜かしら` in [[Learning/Japanese/Songs/Sayonara no Natsu|さよならの夏]].
- **<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してみよう (omoidashite miyou):** The answer in the next line: "let's try to remember" (`〜てみる` + volitional).

---

### Chorus: Asking Doraemon

> ドラえもん　そのポケットで　かなえさせてね
> _Doraemon, with that pocket of yours, let me make it come true_

#### 🔑 Vocab & Points

- **かなえる (<ruby>叶<rt>かな</rt></ruby>える - kanaeru):** To make (a wish/dream) come true. Transitive; the intransitive pair is `叶う` (the dream comes true).
- **かなえさせて (kanaesasete):** Causative `させる` + `て` request: "let me / allow me to make it come true". A child asking for help without asking Doraemon to do it for them.
- **<ruby>輝<rt>かがや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby> (kagayaku yume):** A shining dream (verb directly modifying a noun).
- **<ruby>溢<rt>あふ</rt></ruby>れさせて (afuresasete):** Causative of `溢れる` (to overflow): "make dreams overflow all over the world". The second chorus ends on this.
- **<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>をつないで (te wo tsunaide):** Holding hands (`つなぐ`).

---

### Verse 2: Just Go

> <ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>わないで　<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>履<rt>は</rt></ruby>いて　<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけよう
> _Don't hesitate: put on your shoes and let's head out_

#### 🔑 Vocab & Points

- **<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>わないで (mayowanaide):** `〜ないで` = "without… / don't…". `迷う` means both "to hesitate" and "to get lost", and the song uses both senses.
- **<ruby>履<rt>は</rt></ruby>く (haku):** To put on (shoes, trousers, anything below the waist). Hats use `かぶる`, shirts use `着る`.
- **<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけよう (dekakeyou):** Volitional of `出かける`: "let's go out".
- **<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>さ (daijoubu sa):** "It's alright!" `さ` is a casual, breezy sentence ending.
- **キラキラ (kirakira):** Onomatopoeia for sparkling/glittering.
- **<ruby>宝物<rt>たからもの</rt></ruby> (takaramono):** Treasure.

---

### Bridge 2: Doraemon Answers

> <ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>っても　<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>かないでいいよ
> _Even if you get lost, you don't need to cry_
>
> <ruby>秘密<rt>ひみつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>で　<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>けてあげるよ
> _I'll help you out with my secret gadgets_

#### 🔑 Vocab & Points

- **<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>う (michi ni mayou):** To lose one's way.
- **〜ても (temo):** "Even if…".
- **<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>かないでいい (nakanaide ii):** "It's fine not to cry / you don't have to cry". A gentler cousin of `〜なくてもいい`.
- **<ruby>秘密<rt>ひみつ</rt></ruby><ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby> (himitsu dougu):** "Secret gadgets", the official name for Doraemon's tools.
- **<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>けてあげる (tasukete ageru):** `〜てあげる` = doing a favour for someone. Here the voice switches from the child to Doraemon.

---

### Chorus 2 & Final Bridge

> <ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>になっても　きっと<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れない
> _Even when I grow up, I surely won't forget_

#### 🔑 Vocab & Points

- **<ruby>口笛<rt>くちぶえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>吹<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>く (kuchibue wo fuku):** To whistle.
- **<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>らかに (takaraka ni):** Loudly, proudly, in high spirits.
- **<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>そう (arukidasou):** "Let's start walking", `〜出す` (begin) + volitional. Kiki's song has the same `歩きだす` in [[Learning/Japanese/Songs/Meguru Kisetsu|めぐる季節]].
- **<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けばいいね (todokeba ii ne):** "I hope it reaches (that town)". `〜ばいい` expresses a wish.
- **<ruby>僕等<rt>ぼくら</rt></ruby> (bokura):** We / us (boyish).
- **キミがいれば (kimi ga ireba):** "If you're here", `いる` + `ば`.
- **<ruby>笑顔<rt>えがお</rt></ruby> (egao):** Smiling face.

#### 💭 Deep Interpretation

The bridges form a conversation across the song. Bridge 1 asks `忘れちゃうのかな？` (will I forget?), and the final bridge answers it with `〜ても` instead of `〜たら`: not "when I grow up" but "even if I grow up", I won't forget. The question in the first bridge becomes a promise in the last.

## 📖 Key Japanese Grammar & Expressions

### 1. `〜たら` vs `〜ても`: The Song's Before and After

- <ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>に**なったら** → _when I grow up_ (condition, with worry)
- <ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>に**なっても** → _even if I grow up_ (concession, with confidence)

### 2. Causative Requests: `〜させて(ね)`

`Verb-causative + て` asks for permission to do something yourself.

- かなえ**させて**ね → _let me make it come true_
- <ruby>溢<rt>あふ</rt></ruby>れ**させて** → _make (dreams) overflow_
- Everyday: <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え**させて**ください (_let me think about it_).

### 3. Volitional `〜よう / 〜おう`: "Let's…"

Five in one song: <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してみ**よう**, <ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>をうた**おう**, <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけ**よう**, <ruby>探<rt>さが</rt></ruby>そ**う**よ, <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>そ**う**.

### 4. Casual Contraction: `〜ちゃう` = `〜てしまう`

- <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れて**しまう** → <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ**ちゃう** (_end up forgetting_)
- <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べちゃった (_I ate it all, oops_).

### 5. Giving a Favour: `〜てあげる`

- <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>けて**あげる** (_I'll help you_). Said by the giver; the receiver would say <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>けて**もらう** / **くれる**.

### 6. Wishing: `〜ばいい(ね)`

- <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>け**ばいいね** (_I hope it reaches_). Also `〜といいね` in conversation.
