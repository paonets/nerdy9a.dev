## Summary

The opening keynote on Thailand's place in a fragmenting world and how businesses, especially SMEs, can stay competitive. The framing is **three Ds** (decoupling, diversification, decarbonisation) and a **K-shaped economy** where those who adopt digital tools and AI pull ahead. The government's plan rests on digital government, upskilling, a shift from a tourism to a **visitor economy**, and ASEAN-wide digital rules.

---

## Details

### A decoupling world

- The world is fragmenting into multiple power centres with different ways of thinking: **multipolar**.
- As a smaller country, Thailand has to find its strategic position in that divided world.

### Thailand's economic snapshot

- Exports were roughly **11.1 trillion baht** last year.
- **China and the US together take about one in every three baht** of Thai exports, a significant concentration risk.
- Private-sector investment interest in Thailand is rising to record levels.
- Thailand ranks **#37 on the IMD World Digital Competitiveness** ranking, framed as room to climb rather than a gap.
- SMEs are still **"followers"** in digital capability, not yet competitive leaders.

### The three Ds

| D                   | What it means for business                                                                        |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Decoupling**      | Navigate a fragmented, multipolar global order                                                    |
| **Diversification** | Reduce dependence on a few trade partners; diversify markets and business models                  |
| **Decarbonisation** | Floods, El Niño and fires are reshaping regulation; carbon rules are becoming mandatory worldwide |

### The K-shaped economy

- Even within the same industry there are now clear winners and losers.
- Businesses and people who use digital tools, AI and technology can compete and grow; those who don't risk being pushed out of their sector.
- Example: food. There are countless coffee shops, restaurants and delivery services, and technology is what differentiates the ones that succeed.

### Government initiatives

- **Digital government:** connecting government data systems, e.g. using filed financial statements to verify that a registered business is genuine.
- **DBD Academy:** built with several agencies, led by the Department of Business Development, to upskill founders in financial management and SME operations.
- Three transformation pillars: **people, process and business transformation**.

### From a tourism economy to a visitor economy

- Instead of treating every arrival as a "tourist", understand why they come: medical care, education, remote work, seasonal living.
- Position Thailand as a **digital destination** that attracts talent and knowledge workers, not only holidaymakers.

### ASEAN digital leadership

- Thailand is leading ASEAN's digital agenda and pushing the **Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA)**.
- DEFA would give all 10 ASEAN members shared digital governance rules, making ASEAN the first region in the world with a common digital framework.

### Closing vision

Three things Thailand needs:

1. An economy aligned with climate action.
2. Globally competitive, creative businesses.
3. Thai people with future-ready skills.

### Calls to action for businesses

- Use digital and AI tools to differentiate, especially in crowded sectors like food and services.
- Upgrade your organisation's digital infrastructure.
- Prepare for decarbonisation rules before they bind.
- Adopt cybersecurity and data-governance practices before regulation forces you to; disinformation and misinformation are growing risks.
